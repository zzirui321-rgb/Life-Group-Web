Turn evolving data into interpretable modes.

A movie of a wave often contains several rhythms at once. The Koopman operator perspective and dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) help separate modes from successive snapshots, revealing their frequencies, growth or decay, and spatial profiles.

Our work

03 / TOPOLOGY & LOCALIZATIONPublished

Why do waves spread—or stay localized?

Topology asks how global structure shapes wave behavior. Localization asks how waves remain confined. We aim to connect these physical questions with dynamical features in data.

Physical foundation · Many-body localization and Fermi liquids

In work co-first-authored by Jinze He, we propose a phenomenological correspondence between extended Fermi-liquid quasiparticles and local integrals of motion in many-body localization, and discuss normal-state instabilities. This offers physical clues for comparing many-body states and their observable features.

Read the localization paper ↗

From physical questions to data methods

Which observables help distinguish extended and localized behavior? What can finite-time, finite-size data tell us? These questions connect to data-driven topology and localization; the JPCM paper supplies a physical discussion, not an AI reconstruction algorithm.

04 / SCIENCE FOR AI

What does a neural operator actually learn?

Predicting the next frame is a starting point. We also ask how a network uses physical information, whether its learned structure can be explained, and whether physical operators and wave propagation can become trainable information processors.

Public preprint

MH-RG DeepONet · Bringing physical information into learning

A neural operator learns a mapping between functions. Here, the initial field and coordinates enter branch and trunk networks. Physical descriptors inform both representations before they are combined to predict field evolution. The multi-head structure offers a way to investigate model behavior.

Questions we are pursuing

How does physics help learning?

Physical descriptors can bring relevant structure into a model. We ask when they improve prediction or generalization, and which information actually matters.

How can we understand neural operators?

Can modes, symmetries and multiple scales explain how a model organizes information? Interpretability needs controlled comparisons and testable mechanisms, beyond visualization alone.

Can physical operators be intelligent?

If intelligence means trainable, adaptive information processing, what computations can waves and physical interactions perform? PINED-Net explores this possibility; the broader question remains open.
